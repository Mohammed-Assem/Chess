Every great game starts with the basics.

Learn the board, meet your pieces, and make your first move.

Set the board.

Place the board with a light square in each player’s bottom-right corner. Each side starts with 16 pieces.

From left to right, the back row is rook, knight, bishop, queen, king, bishop, knight, rook. The queen starts on her own color. Eight pawns line up in front.

White moves first. Players then alternate turns, making one legal move at a time.

Know your pieces.

How each piece moves
PieceMovement
KingOne square in any direction. It cannot move onto an attacked square.
QueenAny number of squares in a straight line: horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.
RookAny number of squares horizontally or vertically.
BishopAny number of squares diagonally. It stays on the same color.
KnightAn L-shape: two squares in one direction, then one at a right angle. It can jump over pieces.
PawnOne square forward, or optionally two from its starting square if both squares are empty. Captures one square diagonally forward.

Except for the knight, pieces cannot jump over other pieces. Capture an opponent’s piece by moving onto its square, with the en passant exception below.

Find checkmate.

Check

Your king is under attack. You must escape the check by moving the king, capturing the attacker, or blocking the attack. You may never leave your king in check.

Checkmate

A king is in check and there is no legal escape. The game ends, and the player giving checkmate wins. The king is never actually captured.

Stalemate & draws

If a player is not in check but has no legal move, the game is a draw. Games can also be drawn by agreement, a dead position, or the repetition and move-count rules.

Three special moves.

Castling

Move your king two squares toward a rook, then place that rook on the square the king crossed. Neither piece may have moved before, and the squares between them must be empty. You cannot castle out of, through, or into check.

Promotion

When a pawn reaches the farthest rank, replace it with a queen, rook, bishop, or knight of the same color. You can choose a queen even if you already have one.

En passant

If an enemy pawn advances two squares from its starting square and lands beside your pawn, your pawn may capture it as though it had moved only one square. This capture must happen on your very next move.
